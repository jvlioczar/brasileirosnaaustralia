# Brasileiros na Austrália (Brazilians in Australia)

> Australia for Brazilians: the people making content about life here, the study agencies, the everyday apps and a step-by-step guide for anyone making the move.

To get to know both countries better, besides exploring the other sections of this site, visit the [Australian Culture](https://australianculture.biblioverse.org/) and [Brazilian Culture](https://brazilianculture.biblioverse.org/) projects.

Other language: [Conteúdo completo em português](https://brasileirosnaaustralia.biblioverse.org/llms-full.txt). Updated 14 November 2026 General information only. It's not a substitute for advice from a registered migration agent (MARA), an accountant or a lawyer.

## Creators

Brazilians creating content about life in Australia and, under the Australians filter, a few Aussies with a Brazilian heart. Filter by city, content type or who's behind it; a creator can show up under more than one type. Tap a ticket to open their profile and find people to inspire you!

Link: https://brasileirosnaaustralia.biblioverse.org/?lang=en#criadores

## Facts about Australia

The essentials to know before you arrive: geography, people, government, economy, nature and sport, with up-to-date official data.

Link: https://brasileirosnaaustralia.biblioverse.org/?lang=en#fatos

### Key numbers

- **27.9m** people (Mar 2026)
- **7.69m km²** 6th largest country
- **6 + 2** states and territories
- **Canberra** national capital
- **A$** Australian dollar (AUD)
- **300+** languages spoken
- **32%** born overseas
- **600+** national parks

### Name and symbols

The official name is the Commonwealth of Australia. The national colours, green and gold, were proclaimed in 1984 and are defined today as Pantone 348C and 116C.

### Language

English is the main language (Australia has no official language in law), but more than 300 languages are spoken. At the 2021 Census, 28% of people spoke another language at home, and the most common were Mandarin, Arabic, Vietnamese, Cantonese and Punjabi.

### Location and size

It sits in the southern hemisphere between the Indian and Pacific oceans, in Oceania. At 7.69 million km² it's the world's 6th largest country and the only one that covers a whole continent: the biggest island and the smallest continent.

### States and capitals

Six states: New South Wales (Sydney), Victoria (Melbourne), Queensland (Brisbane), Western Australia (Perth), South Australia (Adelaide) and Tasmania (Hobart). Two territories: the Australian Capital Territory (Canberra, the national capital) and the Northern Territory (Darwin).

### Population

About 27.9 million people (ABS, March 2026), more than five times New Zealand. Only Sydney, Melbourne, Brisbane, Perth and Adelaide have over a million people, and all of them are on the coast.

### Diversity

People from nearly 200 nationalities live here: 32% of the population was born overseas (ABS, June 2025). At the 2021 Census, 36.7% had both parents born overseas, and 812,728 people (3.2%) identified as Aboriginal and/or Torres Strait Islander.

### Brazilians in Australia

Brazil and Australia have had diplomatic relations since 1945. According to the ABS, about 74,000 Australian residents were born in Brazil (June 2025), and the 2021 Census counted almost 40,000 people of Brazilian ancestry. The 2026 Census was held on 11 August, with first results due around June 2027.

### Government

It's a federal parliamentary democracy based on the British model and led by the Prime Minister. It's also a constitutional monarchy: the King of the United Kingdom is head of state in a symbolic role, and many Australians would like to become a republic.

### History

Aboriginal people arrived around 60,000 years ago and form one of the oldest continuous living cultures on Earth. Dutch explorers landed in 1606 and the British founded a penal colony in 1788. The gold rush began in 1851 and in 1901 the six colonies joined together in Federation.

### Reconciliation

Since the 1960s the country has worked to address injustices against Indigenous peoples. In 2008 the government formally apologised to the Stolen Generations, and National Sorry Day, on 26 May, remembers those wrongs.

### Economy

An open, diversified economy that grew for 28 years in a row before the pandemic. It exports iron ore, gold, coal and gas, as well as wool, wheat, beef and wine. International education is the biggest services export, and China is the main trading partner.

### Education

School runs for 13 years, from Prep to Year 12, and is compulsory until at least 16. There are 43 universities, and 9 of them are in the world's top 100 in the QS 2027 rankings (UNSW leads the country at 19th). It's one of the most popular destinations for international students.

### Health and quality of life

The public system, Medicare, is among the most efficient in the world. Life expectancy is 85 for women and 81 for men, and the country regularly ranks near the top of indexes like the HDI and the Global Peace Index.

### Nature and climate

The climate ranges from tropical in the north to temperate in the south, with the arid outback in the middle and snow in the south-east's mountains. Only 6% of the land is arable. Uluru stands 348 m tall, and the Great Artesian Basin is the world's largest underground water reserve.

### Biodiversity

Around 10% of the world's biodiversity lives here, much of it found nowhere else: more than 140 marsupial species such as kangaroos, koalas, wombats and Tasmanian devils, plus the platypus. Marine protected areas cover 37% of its waters, including the Great Barrier Reef, the world's largest reef. The country has more than 600 national parks.

### Lifestyle and sport

The Aussie way of life is laid-back and welcoming, with plenty of outdoor living, beaches and barbies. Surfing, cricket, rugby and Aussie rules (AFL) are national passions, and soccer, basketball, netball, tennis and Formula 1 are hugely popular too.

### Olympic Games

Australia has hosted two Summer Olympics, Melbourne 1956 and Sydney 2000, which also hosted the Paralympics. Brisbane will host the 2032 Games.

### Formula 1

The Australian Grand Prix was held on the streets of Adelaide from 1985 to 1995. Since 1996 it has been held at Albert Park in Melbourne, often as the season opener.

### World Cups

Australia co-hosted the record-breaking 2023 FIFA Women's World Cup with New Zealand. It also hosted the 2003 Rugby World Cup, the 1992 and 2015 Cricket World Cups (with New Zealand) and the 2022 T20 World Cup. Next up are the men's Rugby World Cup in 2027 and the women's in 2029.

## Arrival guide

In the order things happen, from Brazil to your first car. Figures checked in October 2026. Rules change, so always double-check on the official website.

Link: https://brasileirosnaaustralia.biblioverse.org/?lang=en#guia

### 1. Before you fly

_Visa, health cover and leaving Brazil's tax system_

#### Student visa (subclass 500)

Base application charge from 1 July 2026: A$2,050 for English courses (ELICOS) and A$2,500 for higher education, postgrad and VET courses. While your course is in session you can work up to 48 hours per fortnight; during official course breaks there's no limit.

#### Work and Holiday (subclass 462)

For Brazilians aged 18 to 30, with up to 500 places a year. You'll need a completed degree or at least two years of uni, functional English and enough money to support yourself. Places reopen every 2 July, and once the cap is reached the government pauses applications. Keep an eye on the Home Affairs caps page.

#### Proof of funds: is it compulsory?

Having the money is compulsory; attaching proof depends on your case. For a student visa, the official benchmark is A$29,710 a year for living costs (pro rata for shorter courses), plus your first year of course fees and travel. Add A$10,394 for a partner and A$4,449 for each child. With a level 1 or 2 provider, Brazilians usually don't need to attach bank statements when applying, but Home Affairs can ask for them at any time; level 3 providers require them. For the Work and Holiday visa, proof is required: around A$5,000, plus enough for a return flight.

#### Brazil's current evidence level

Under the Simplified Student Visa Framework, Brazil has been at evidence level 2 since April 2025, when it dropped from level 1 after a rise in refusals. The scale runs from 1 (fewer documents) to 3 (more documents) and is reviewed regularly. Before you apply, check what applies to you with the Home Affairs Document Checklist Tool, entering your passport and your provider.

#### Health cover is compulsory

Brazil doesn't have a reciprocal health care agreement with Medicare. Students need OSHC for the whole length of their visa; other visas usually require OVHC.

#### In Brazil, before you leave

- Decide whether you'll lodge the **Comunicação e Declaração de Saída Definitiva do País** (notice of permanent departure) with the Receita Federal. Without it, you stay a Brazilian tax resident and keep filing your tax return there.
- Leave a power of attorney with someone you trust and keep your CPF in good standing.
- Keep your Brazilian bank account open: Pix, gov.br and the banks will still rely on it.
- Bring your degrees, academic transcripts and Brazilian driver's licence (CNH), ideally with English translations.

Sources: Home Affairs (immi.homeaffairs.gov.au), SBS Portuguese, Receita Federal.

### 2. First week

_SIM card, bank, TFN and transport card_

#### SIM card

Telstra, Optus and Vodafone run the three networks. Brands like Boost (on the Telstra network), Amaysim and Felix are prepaid and usually cheaper. All you need is your passport.

#### Bank account

CommBank, NAB, Westpac and ANZ are the big four. Most of them let you open an account online before you arrive and verify your passport at a branch. To send and receive reais, Wise and Revolut usually have the best exchange rates.

#### TFN (Tax File Number)

Your tax number, which you need so you don't pay tax at the top rate. Apply on the ATO website as soon as you land. It's **free**: never pay a middleman for it.

### 3. Work

_Minimum wage, super and your rights_

#### National minimum wage

A$26.44 an hour from 1 July 2026. Casual workers get 25% on top (casual loading). Many jobs have a higher minimum under their award: use the Fair Work pay calculator.

#### Superannuation

Your employer pays 12% of your wages into a retirement fund (super). If you leave Australia for good, you can claim it back (DASP).

#### ABN

To work as a sole trader (delivery, your own cleaning business, freelance gigs) you need an ABN, which is also free, from the Australian Business Register. Tax and insurance then become your responsibility.

#### If you're paid below the minimum

Your visa doesn't stop you from making a complaint. The Fair Work Ombudsman helps workers on temporary visas and offers a free interpreter.

Sources: Fair Work Ombudsman (fairwork.gov.au), ATO.

### 4. Housing

_Rooms, renting and bond_

The first step is usually a room in a share house (Flatmates, Gumtree, Brazilian community groups). To rent a whole place, the main sites are realestate.com.au and Domain.

- The **bond** is usually up to 4 weeks' rent and must be lodged with the state authority, not kept by the landlord.
- Fill in the entry condition report with photos. It protects your bond when you move out.
- Never pay anything before you've inspected the place or spoken to whoever is advertising it. Room scams are common.

### 5. Health

_Insurance, GPs and emergencies_

Without Medicare, your cover is your insurance (OSHC or OVHC). Before booking an appointment, check whether the clinic offers direct billing with your insurer, so you don't have to pay upfront and claim it back.

- To find a GP, dentist or tests: HealthEngine or HotDoc.
- Not sure if you need to go to emergency? Call Healthdirect (24/7, staffed by nurses).
- A genuine emergency: 000.

### 6. Tax

_Your Australian tax return and your situation in Brazil_

#### In Australia

The financial year runs from 1 July to 30 June. If you worked, lodge your return by 31 October through myGov linked to the ATO. With a registered tax agent, you usually get more time. Lots of people get a refund, so keep receipts for work-related expenses.

- Working holiday makers are taxed at their own rates (15% up to A$45,000).
- Students and WHV holders are often treated differently from residents: check your situation with the ATO.

#### In Brazil

If you **didn't** lodge the permanent departure notice, you still have to file your IRPF return in Brazil and declare your overseas income, offsetting the tax already paid in Australia under the Receita Federal's rules.

Sources: ATO (ato.gov.au), Receita Federal. If in doubt, talk to an accountant who works with Brazilians.

### 7. Cars

_Driving, buying and transferring_

#### Can I drive on my Brazilian licence?

On a temporary visa, generally yes, as long as your CNH is current and in English or comes with an official translation (or an International Driving Permit). Rules and time limits vary by state: check with the transport authority (TMR in QLD, Service NSW, VicRoads, Transport WA).

#### Buying a used car

- Ask for the VIN and run a **PPSR** check (ppsr.gov.au). It shows whether the car has money owing, has been stolen or written off. It costs a few dollars and saves you from the biggest scam in the market.
- Check the **rego** (registration), which in most states already includes compulsory third party (CTP) insurance for personal injury.
- Ask for the inspection certificate where your state requires one: a Safety Certificate in QLD, an RWC in Victoria, a pink slip in NSW.
- Get at least third party property insurance. CTP doesn't cover other people's cars.

#### Transfer

The seller and buyer both sign the transfer form, and the buyer pays the fee and stamp duty within the state's deadline (14 days in Queensland). In several states you can do it all online.

### 8. Documents and government

_myGov, myID, VEVO and the consulate_

- **myGov**: one portal linking the ATO, Medicare and Centrelink. Set up your account early.
- **myID**: the government's digital ID, used to sign in to myGov and the ATO.
- **VEVO**: the official check of your visa and its conditions. Employers may ask for it.
- **100 points of ID**: banks, rentals and SIM cards ask for documents adding up to 100 points. Your passport is worth the most.
- **Consulate-General of Brazil in Sydney** and the **Embassy in Canberra**: passports, powers of attorney, birth registration and voter ID.

### 9. Renewing your visa

_A new student visa, a second WHV year and what changed in 2026_

#### Start before your visa runs out

Plan at least 2 to 3 months ahead. If you're allowed to apply onshore and you lodge before your visa expires, you'll usually get a **bridging visa** that keeps your current conditions while the new one is processed. First, check VEVO for condition **8503 (No Further Stay)**: if your visa has it, you can't apply for another visa while you're here.

#### A new student visa (500): what changed on 2 October 2026

For applications made from 2 October 2026, current student visa holders applying for another one as the main applicant generally have to **apply from outside Australia** and be offshore when it's granted. You can still apply onshore in a few cases, such as:

- up to **12 extra months** to finish the same main course, usually with the same provider;
- **moving up a level** (AQF) after finishing your course, say from a diploma to a bachelor's, or from a non-AQF course (like English) to an AQF one;
- a PhD, primary or secondary school, DFAT or Defence sponsorship, or your provider closing down (provider default).

People on a **WHV (417 or 462)**, a visitor visa (600) or a Temporary Graduate visa (485) can't switch to a student visa onshore any more either. Applications lodged before 2 October stay under the old rules.

#### What a new student visa needs

- A **CoE** (Confirmation of Enrolment) for a CRICOS-registered course;
- **Genuine Student**: showing you really intend to study, with a study history that makes sense, plus attendance and results from your current course;
- **Funds**: A$29,710 a year for living costs, plus fees and travel (when evidence is requested);
- **OSHC** for the whole new stay, **English** where required, and the visa charge (see step 1).

#### A second and third Work and Holiday visa (462)

- **2nd year**: 3 months (88 days) of specified work on your first visa.
- **3rd year**: 6 months of specified work on your second visa.
- It has to be in the regional areas the government lists: farming, fishing, mining and construction, plus tourism and hospitality in northern and remote Australia. Check the postcode on the official list before you take the job.
- The work must be paid at award rates, with payslips and an employer reference. Cash-in-hand work doesn't count.
- You still need to be aged 18 to 30 on the day you apply for your second or third visa.

#### After you graduate

If you've finished a CRICOS course of at least 2 years, look at the **Temporary Graduate visa (485)**, applied for onshore within 6 months of finishing. Right now the age limit is 35 in most cases and the minimum English is IELTS 6.5 or equivalent.

Sources: Home Affairs (immi.homeaffairs.gov.au), Migration Amendment (Student Visa Reform) Regulations 2026 and LIN 26/102. Visa rules change often: if in doubt, talk to a registered migration agent (MARA).

## Frequently asked questions

Short answers based on the guide above. Figures checked in October 2026; rules change, so confirm on the official website.

Link: https://brasileirosnaaustralia.biblioverse.org/?lang=en#faq

### How much does an Australian student visa cost?

Since 1 July 2026 the base fee is A$2,050 for English courses (ELICOS) and A$2,500 for degrees, postgraduate and vocational courses. On top of the fee you need OSHC (health cover) and proof of living costs.

### How many hours can a student work in Australia?

Up to 48 hours per fortnight while classes are on. There is no limit during official course breaks.

### What is the minimum wage in Australia?

A$26.44 an hour since 1 July 2026. Casual workers get 25% extra (casual loading) and the employer pays 12% of your wage into superannuation. Many jobs have a higher floor under their award.

### Can Brazilians get a Work and Holiday visa for Australia?

Yes. The Work and Holiday visa (subclass 462) is for Brazilians aged 18 to 30, with up to 500 places a year that reopen on 2 July. It requires a completed degree or at least two years of university, functional English and enough money to support yourself.

### How much money do I need to show for a student visa?

The official benchmark is A$29,710 a year for living costs, plus the first year of the course and the airfare. A partner adds A$10,394 and each child A$4,449. Having the money is mandatory; attaching bank statements depends on the school's evidence level, and Brazil has been at level 2 since April 2025.

### Do I need health insurance in Australia?

Yes. Brazil has no reciprocal agreement with Medicare. Students need OSHC for the whole visa; other temporary visas usually require OVHC. In an emergency, call 000.

### Which Australian city is best for Brazilians?

It depends on what you want. Sydney has the most jobs and the biggest Brazilian community but is the most expensive; the Gold Coast has the highest rent and one of the largest communities; Melbourne and Adelaide are among the cheapest capitals; Perth, Adelaide, Canberra, Gold Coast and Sunshine Coast, among others, count as designated regional areas for visas. The Which city? tab compares 12 cities.

### Can I drive in Australia with a Brazilian licence?

On a temporary visa, generally yes, as long as the licence is valid and in English or with an official translation (or accompanied by an International Driving Permit). Rules and time limits vary by state.

### What is a TFN and how do I get one?

The Tax File Number is your tax number; without it you pay the top tax rate. It is free and you apply on the ATO website as soon as you arrive. Never pay a middleman for it.

### How many Brazilians live in Australia?

According to the ABS, around 74,000 Australian residents were born in Brazil (June 2025), and the 2021 Census recorded almost 40,000 people of Brazilian ancestry.
